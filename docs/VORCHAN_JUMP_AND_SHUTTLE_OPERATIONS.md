# Vorchan Jump And Shuttle Operations

Implemented 24 November 2026 from the supplied Second Edition Rules Compendium and official Vorchan Warship Version 3 SCS.

## Sources

PDF page numbers: initiative and launch penalties 13; shuttle movement 31–32; hangar and jump-engine critical effects 64; hangars and launch/recovery 135–136; jump engines, recharge and opening 141; entry, closure and maintenance 142. The Vorchan SCS supplies jump delay 16, hangar launch/recovery rate 2, and two unarmed shuttles, each with thrust 4, armour 0, forward/aft defence 8, side defence 10 and 10 damage boxes. The actual jump engine has 16 boxes; explosion probability is the damaged percentage rounded down.

## Tracked Play

- Jump Point Formation follows EW and precedes movement. Choose a legal clear-space map hex within four hexes and an immutable entry side. Opening has no additional power charge. A forming token becomes open at turn end and is usable from the following turn. Any unit may enter voluntarily through the correct hex side. Movement ends on entry and the unit disengages from this scenario. A multi-hex command carrying an entry choice stops at the point rather than passing through it.
- Entry uses the actual previous hex, not hull facing. Units already in the hex retain their recorded approach; setup placement alone does not invent an entry side. Use single-hex movement and Enter Jump Point to enter from the UI. The ship that generated the point entering it forces end-of-turn closure.
- End-of-turn closure permits maintenance after the first full usable turn. Four full usable turns is the maximum. Maintaining requires remaining within four hexes, functioning powered jump engines, shutting down weapons and hangar operations, and reserving all spare power; sensors and normal free-thrust manoeuvres remain available. Required closures and phases without decisions complete automatically.
- Closure starts the 16-turn recharge. Voluntary jump-engine deactivation is prohibited until more than half its boxes are damaged. Any deactivation, including reactor shutdown, requires 160 turns of recharge after reactivation. Damaged engines roll a server d100 on opening and each future formation segment while holding the point. The saved result is public, with a visible result panel and journal entry; a failed check destroys the ship.
- Hangar Operations occurs after criticals and vortex closure. Launch/recovery selections are validated against the shared rate, surviving hangar boxes, powered availability, carrier pivot/roll restrictions, and ownership. Destruction of an occupied hangar box requires a real casualty choice if alternative shuttles could be lost. Complete hangar/carrier destruction automatically loses all aboard shuttles.
- Launch places a shuttle in the carrier hex with inherited facing, signed speed and inertial axis. No movement or attack occurs on the launch turn. Next-turn initiative applies −4 to the carrier and −10 to each newly launched shuttle, with a separate authoritative initiative roll per deployed unit. Movement order includes ships and shuttles, not simply one action per player.
- Shuttle movement tracks four thrust, speed changes before movement, forward travel, turns at one-third speed with delay and optional shortening, extended turns, slides with intervening normal travel, instantaneous pivots and rolls. A 180° pivot preserves physical travel. Optional combat pivots after all movement cost three thrust per facing change. Unarmed shuttles do not jink, snap-turn, purchase thrust or fire weapons.
- Recovery requires the same hex and direction of travel, with shuttle speed between carrier speed and carrier speed plus four. Launched and recovered inventory retains damage. A carrier's two shuttles share an offensive EW target group, following the up-to-six individual-shuttle lock-on rule.
- Vorchan weapons can target deployed enemy shuttles with the shuttle defence and fighter/shuttle fire-control column. Successful hits roll damage directly against the ten-box track without ship location tables. Armour is zero, damage is persisted and destruction removes the map unit. Optional fighter critical rules remain disabled; these shuttles are unarmed, and this scenario does not add boarding, cargo missions or combat in hyperspace.

## Presentation And Compatibility

Both map renderers show separate shuttle icons and oriented jump-point tokens, with forming/open state and entry side. Find controls include those units. The SCS view lists shuttle status and live ten-box tracks. The shuttle's final 3D asset is pending the user's model; the 3D map currently uses a small arrow/cone token.

Production new battles enable operations. Existing saved battles initialise the previously unused shuttle/jump state without clearing users, battles or damage, including an existing movement order. Legacy reducer fixtures can continue without operations; authenticated API coverage enables the production path. All objects live in the revision-protected battle JSON and event journal, so no database migration or account reset is needed.

Validation covers rules, ownership, same-tier shuttle damage, launch and next-turn movement, damaged-engine risk, closure/recharge, maintenance power, recovery, hangar casualties, extended turns and authenticated reload persistence. Production React components were server-rendered to check jump, hangar, shuttle movement, power, SCS and 2D token screens. Browser automation was unavailable in this session; interactive visual testing, especially WebGL and narrow mobile layout, remains a practical follow-up.
